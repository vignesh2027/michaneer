# Michaneer evaluation

Generated by `uv run python -m eval.run_eval`. Inputs are published figures (see michaneer/sources.py) or grids.

## 1. The salt balance closes

Grid: 8 tap TDS values from 50 to 6000 mg/L, 10 recovery levels from 10 to 90 percent, 5 membrane salt rejections from 80 to 99 percent: 400 points.

Largest relative error between salt in and salt out: 1.5e-16. The reject TDS formula conserves mass to floating point precision.

## 2. Litres down the drain per person per year

One person drinking and cooking with 7.5 L of purified water a day (WHO, Howard and Bartram 2003).

| Recovery level | Recovery | Reject per litre | Reject per person per year |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| IS 16240:2015 floor (superseded) | 20% | 4.00 L | 10,950 L |
| 3 L reject per litre, top of the Lok Sabha range | 25% | 3.00 L | 8,212 L |
| 2 L reject per litre, middle of the Lok Sabha range | 33% | 2.00 L | 5,475 L |
| IS 16240:2023 floor | 40% | 1.50 L | 4,106 L |
| 1 L reject per litre, bottom of the Lok Sabha range | 50% | 1.00 L | 2,738 L |
| NGT direction (stayed by the Supreme Court) | 60% | 0.67 L | 1,825 L |

Finding: across the official range, one person's purifier sends between 2,738 and 8,212 litres a year to the drain. Bringing a unit at the top of the range up to the 2023 floor saves 4,106 litres per person per year, before any reuse. The spread is a factor of three, which is why the app measures instead of assuming a ratio.

## 3. How precise is a kitchen bucket test?

One litre of purified water; the only error is reading the bucket on a jug marked every 50 or 100 mL (half a marking either way).

| True recovery | Reject collected | Range, 50 mL jug | Range, 100 mL jug |
| ---: | ---: | --- | --- |
| 20% | 4,000 mL | 19.9 to 20.1% | 19.8 to 20.2% |
| 25% | 3,000 mL | 24.8 to 25.2% | 24.7 to 25.3% |
| 33% | 2,000 mL | 33.1 to 33.6% | 32.8 to 33.9% |
| 40% | 1,500 mL | 39.6 to 40.4% | 39.2 to 40.8% |
| 50% | 1,000 mL | 49.4 to 50.6% | 48.8 to 51.3% |
| 60% | 667 mL | 59.1 to 60.9% | 58.3 to 61.9% |

Finding: even with a coarse 100 mL jug the error band is under 3.6 percentage points, so a 25 percent unit and a 40 percent unit never overlap. The bucket test is precise enough for the decision it supports. Larger real-world errors come from starting and stopping the bottle and hose at different moments and from pressure changes, which is why the app asks for a repeat.

## 4. Is "water your plants with it" good advice?

Highest tap-water TDS for which the reject stream stays under FAO's 450 mg/L no-restriction line (FAO Irrigation and Drainage Paper 29, Table 1), from the salt balance.

| Recovery | 90% salt rejection | 95% salt rejection | 98% salt rejection |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 20% | 367 mg/L | 364 mg/L | 361 mg/L |
| 25% | 346 mg/L | 342 mg/L | 339 mg/L |
| 33% | 310 mg/L | 305 mg/L | 302 mg/L |
| 40% | 281 mg/L | 276 mg/L | 272 mg/L |
| 50% | 237 mg/L | 231 mg/L | 227 mg/L |
| 60% | 191 mg/L | 186 mg/L | 182 mg/L |

Finding: at 25 percent recovery and 95 percent rejection, any tap water above 342 mg/L gives reject water outside FAO's no-restriction class. Tap water right at the IS 10500 acceptable limit of 500 mg/L becomes about 658 mg/L in the reject stream. Blanket advice to pour RO reject on plants is only safe for low-TDS supplies, so the app gives plant advice only from the computed reject TDS, and assumes the worst case when the purified-water reading is missing.

## 5. Worked example through the app

The app's built-in example (labelled as illustrative in the UI): 3000 mL reject per 1000 mL purified, the top of the Lok Sabha range; 30 L a day of purified water; label rating 40 percent; tap 420 mg/L, purified 35 mg/L; 60 L a day of reuse capacity.

* Recovery 25.0 percent, range 24.8 to 25.2.
* 90.0 L of reject a day, 32,850 L a year.
* Reject TDS 548 mg/L, FAO class "slight".
* Plan: Get the unit serviced back to its rating: 16,425 L a year.
* Plan: Catch the reject hose in a bucket and use it: 16,425 L a year.
* Plan: Ask whether you need RO at all (only after a lab test): 32,850 L a year.
* Total for the unconditional steps: 32,850 L a year, which equals all of today's reject water and never more (a property test checks this across 80 input combinations).

## What this evaluation does not show

* No field measurements. The owner has not yet logged a before and after test on a real unit; when he does, the numbers go in docs/SUBMISSION.md under their own heading, never mixed with these.
* No city-wide total. There is no public count of RO purifiers in Chennai, so we do not multiply anything by a guess.
* Recovery drifts with inlet pressure, temperature and filter age. One test is one moment.
