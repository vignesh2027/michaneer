Ten minutes, things you already own

How to run the bucket test

The official range is wide: a Lok Sabha answer puts reject water at 1 to 3 litres per litre of purified water, and the ministry said it had not studied it. Your own purifier sits somewhere in that range, and the test finds where.

You need

  • A clean 1 litre bottle.
  • A bucket big enough for 5 litres.
  • A measuring jug. Kitchen jugs are marked every 50 or 100 mL; enter that gap so the result shows its error range.
  • Optional: a phone stopwatch, a TDS pen, and the purifier's label.

Steps

  1. Most home purifiers send clean water into a storage tank, and the tap drinks from the tank. Empty it first: fill bottles from the tap until the flow drops to a thin stream. With the tap left open, that stream is what the membrane is making right now.
  2. Keep the tap open. Put the bottle under it and the reject hose into the empty bucket at the same moment, and start the stopwatch.
  3. When the bottle reaches its mark, pull the hose out of the bucket and stop the watch.
  4. Pour the bucket into the jug, a jug at a time, and add up the millilitres.
  5. Enter the numbers. A smaller bottle works too; enter its size as the purified amount. Repeat once; if the two results differ by more than the error range, average them.

Common mistakes

  • Testing from a full tank. Stored water pours out fast while the reject hose barely runs, which makes the purifier look far better than it is.
  • Low pressure hours. Recovery falls when the inlet pressure is low. Test at the time you normally fill bottles.
  • Swapped TDS readings. Tap water is the higher number; purified water is the lower one.

What the numbers mean

  • Recovery is purified divided by purified plus reject. BIS IS 16240:2023 asks new domestic purifiers for at least 40 percent; the 2015 edition asked for 20.
  • Reject TDS follows from a salt balance: whatever salt the membrane holds back leaves in the reject stream. Without a purified reading Michaneer assumes all of it does, which gives the highest possible value.
  • Plants use the FAO irrigation classes: under 450 mg/L no restriction, 450 to 2000 slight to moderate, over 2000 severe.

What Michaneer will not tell you

Whether water is safe to drink. A TDS pen measures dissolved salts only. Germs, fluoride, nitrate and arsenic need a lab test, for example at a TWAD Board water testing laboratory in Tamil Nadu.
